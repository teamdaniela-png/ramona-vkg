Abstract

A domain ontology in OWL 2 QL (DL-Lite) for the detection of fraudulent job offers, labour-recruitment fraud, and human-trafficking recruitment patterns in Latin America. RATR-O models the full continuum of recruitment-based harm: from offers that defraud candidates (identity theft, financial scams, unpaid work) through offers that lead to labour exploitation, through to offers whose purpose is trafficking into forced labour, sexual exploitation, or forced criminality. A single JobOffer can exhibit indicators of any combination of these three outcomes, and RATR-O expresses them as distinct but compatible risk axes. The ontology is designed to operate as the conceptual layer of a Virtual Knowledge Graph over Ramona AI operational data and over open anti-trafficking datasets (CTDC, UNODC), following Xiao, Ding, Cogrel and Calvanese (2019). v1.0 aligns the generalisable fragment of HUTRO (Human Trafficking Ontology, BioPortal, 2025) with Ramona's domain-specific taxonomy of online recruitment fraud in Mexico and Latin America.

Classes

:AgeGroup — Age group

es: Grupo etario

A coarse age bucket aligned to CTDC: 0-8, 9-17, 18-20, 21-23, 24-26, 27-29, 30-38, 39-47, 48+.

:Agent — Agent

es: Agente

An actor who can post or be associated with a job offer.

Superclass of:Employer, :Recruiter

:AgriculturalBondageScheme — Agricultural labour bondage scheme

es: Esquema de servidumbre en trabajo agrícola

Offer of seasonal agricultural work (jornalero, harvest, produce packing) that leads to wage theft, deductions for housing and food that exceed the wage, confiscation of identification documents, and restriction of movement through geographic isolation. Documented by ILO, UNODC and the Polaris Project in the US-Mexico corridor (fresh produce, coffee, tomato) and in internal Mexican migration from southern states to Sinaloa, Sonora and Baja California. The CTDC dataset registers :labour_agriculture as a distinct exploitation subtype.

Subclass of:FraudScheme

:BeggingRingScheme — Forced begging ring scheme

es: Esquema de mendicidad forzada

Offer of 'help' or 'a job in the city' that leads to forced begging under the control of a ring leader. Common victims are minors, persons with disabilities, and migrants with precarious status. The ring leader confiscates earnings and controls movement. Named explicitly in the UNODC Protocol against Trafficking in Persons glossary and discussed by UNICEF in urban contexts in Mexico, Honduras and Guatemala. Maps to :PurposeForcedLabour or :PurposeSlavery depending on the context.

Subclass of:FraudScheme

:CallCenterScamScheme — Call-centre scam employment scheme

es: Esquema de empleo en call center estafador

The candidate is hired as a legitimate customer-service, collections or sales agent in a call centre that is in fact a fraud operation (time-share extortion, fake investment, virtual kidnapping, romance scams). The worker realises they are defrauding clients and attempts to leave, at which point threats, wage withholding or confiscated documents prevent them from doing so. Documented in Mexico, Costa Rica and the Philippines.

Subclass of:FraudScheme

:Candidate — Candidate

es: Candidato

User who submitted an offer to Ramona for verification.

:CentralAmericanMigrantLabourScheme — Central-American migrant labour exploitation scheme

es: Esquema de explotación laboral de migrantes centroamericanos

Offer of work in the United States or northern Mexico, targeted at Guatemalan, Honduran or Salvadoran candidates, that routes the victim via Tapachula and the Mexican migration corridor. On arrival, the victim is placed in construction, agriculture or hospitality under conditions of debt bondage: smuggling fees, document retention, threats of denunciation to immigration authorities. CTDC documents the GTM-USA corridor with 842 victims 2009-2023 and the HND and SLV citizenship clusters. Recognised by UNODC GLOTIP as a principal Latin-American corridor.

Subclass of:DebtBondageTraffickingScheme

:ChildRecruitmentCriminalScheme — Child recruitment into criminal organisations

es: Esquema de reclutamiento infantil por grupos delictivos

Recruitment of minors by organised-crime organisations as halcones (lookouts), runners, enforcers, or in the production and logistics of drug trafficking. Extensively documented in Mexico by REDIM (Red por los Derechos de la Infancia en México) in their 2010-2024 series. Recognised by the CNDH and ongoing federal legislative debate. Entry points include offers disguised as 'security', 'warehouse work' or 'deliveries', plus in-school contact and social-media grooming. UNICEF treats recruitment of minors by armed groups as a grave violation.

Subclass of:ForcedCriminalityScheme
Superclass of:GangRecruitmentSchoolsScheme

:ConstructionLabourBondageScheme — Construction labour bondage scheme

es: Esquema de servidumbre en construcción

Offer of construction work abroad or in another state with housing and 'flight to site' included. Upon arrival the worker finds pay deductions for housing, transport and tools that exceed the wage; documents are retained; movement off site is restricted. Documented by ILO in the Gulf States labour corridor (most notoriously Qatar World Cup preparation) and by HRW and Polaris in the US-Mexico corridor for Mexican and Central-American workers. Also attested internally in Mexico for workers displaced from southern states to the northern border.

Subclass of:FraudScheme

:Country — Country

es: País

:CredibleLethalPattern — Credible-lethal pattern

es: Patrón creíble-letal

A survivor-testimony pattern where the offer itself looks plausible and the risk sits in the logistics of the first contact (nameless WhatsApp, multi-use building, 'come alone', 'no ID', Messenger-to-WhatsApp funnel, personal-data filter). Type B in Ramona's dual-track classification.

Subclass of:SurvivorTestimonyPattern

:DebtBondageTraffickingScheme — Debt bondage trafficking scheme

es: Esquema de trata con deuda

The offer relocates the candidate to a destination where exploitation is performed, often across borders, and binds the victim through a manufactured debt: smuggling fees, transportation, housing, paperwork. Covers the Tapachula-to-United States labour-exploitation corridor, the Latin-America-to-Western-Europe sex-trafficking pattern (e.g. falsely advertised childcare jobs in Paris), and domestic-work trafficking with document retention.

Subclass of:FraudScheme
Superclass of:CentralAmericanMigrantLabourScheme, :ForcedChildMarriageScheme

:DomesticWorkerLockInScheme — Domestic worker lock-in scheme

es: Esquema de encierro en trabajo doméstico

The candidate is hired as a live-in domestic worker, caregiver or nanny and then isolated inside the employer's household: documents withheld, phone confiscated or rationed, days-off refused, movement outside the home forbidden. Wages are withheld or paid to a family member. Common end-state of VisaFraudScheme placements abroad and of internal placements from rural to urban Mexico.

Subclass of:FraudScheme

:FakeMaquilaScheme — Fake maquila scheme

es: Esquema de maquila fantasma

Offer of employment at an export-processing factory in the Mexican north border or in border free-trade zones. The maquila is either non-existent (workers arrive to find no facility), a cover for forced labour in a related industry (for example drug packaging, human-trafficking logistics), or a real facility operating under abusive conditions below the legal minimum. Candidates commonly relocate from southern Mexico and Central America.

Subclass of:FraudScheme

:FishingVesselLabourScheme — Fishing vessel labour scheme

es: Esquema de explotación laboral en pesca

Offer of work on deep-sea fishing vessels with promised wages and return dates. Victims are taken to international waters where they are held for months or years without pay, with document confiscation, physical abuse and no possibility of exit. Extensively documented by the International Labour Organization ('Caught at Sea', 2013) and by the Environmental Justice Foundation. Principal victim populations include Myanmar, Cambodia, Indonesia and the Philippines; vessels often registered in Thailand, Taiwan, Korea.

Subclass of:FraudScheme

:ForcedChildMarriageScheme — Forced child marriage scheme

es: Esquema de matrimonio infantil forzado

The offer frames a minor's placement as marriage or an 'informal union' under customary practice (usos y costumbres). The Secretaría de las Mujeres de Oaxaca recognises this explicitly as a form of human trafficking. According to ENADID 2014, 44.82% of women in Chiapas, 42.41% in Guerrero and 39.17% in Oaxaca reported child marriage or early union. Recognised by UNICEF as a harmful practice and by UN Women as a form of gender-based violence. Maps to :PurposeServitude in the Palermo Protocol.

Subclass of:DebtBondageTraffickingScheme

:ForcedCriminalityScheme — Forced criminality scheme

es: Esquema de reclutamiento para criminalidad forzada

Offer advertised as 'private security', 'bodyguard', 'armed escort', 'watchman at a ranch' or 'warehouse security' with atypically high salary. The real purpose is enlistment into organised-crime armed structures, most notably as halcones, sicarios, or coca-processing labour. Archetypal Mexican case: the CJNG Rancho Izaguirre (Teuchitlán, Jalisco) recruiting scheme of 2024-2025.

Subclass of:FraudScheme
Superclass of:ChildRecruitmentCriminalScheme

:ForeignLanguageTeacherScheme — Foreign-language teacher scheme

es: Esquema de maestro de idiomas en el extranjero

The candidate is offered a teaching position abroad (typically Spanish in Asia or English in Latin America) with housing, visa and flight paid by the agency. On arrival the promised contract does not exist: the candidate is placed in a different role, forced to pay back 'travel expenses', has documents retained, or finds the school is a front for sex tourism or forced domestic work. Documented corridors include Mexico-China, Mexico-Korea, and Peru-Japan.

Subclass of:FraudScheme

:FraudRisk — Labour fraud risk

es: Riesgo de fraude laboral

Risk that the offer is a fraud designed to extract money, data or unpaid work from the candidate, without necessarily leading to trafficking or exploitation.

Subclass of:RiskClassification

:FraudScheme — Fraud scheme

es: Esquema de fraude

A named operational pattern by which a fraudulent job offer harms the candidate. Independent of the RiskClassification axes: a single offer can exhibit one or more named schemes.

:GangRecruitmentSchoolsScheme — Gang recruitment at schools scheme

es: Esquema de reclutamiento de pandillas o cárteles en escuelas

Specific subtype of child recruitment where the entry point is the school ground or school exit. Recruiters are often students slightly older than the target or adults posing as relatives. Common in Central America (MS-13, Barrio 18) and in Mexican states with heavy cartel presence (Michoacán, Guerrero, Jalisco, Tamaulipas). The Gobierno de Zapopan published a public prevention programme in 2026. REDIM documented 26 press articles in the current Ramona observatory corpus alone.

Subclass of:ChildRecruitmentCriminalScheme

:GigEconomyFakeDriverScheme — Gig-economy fake driver scheme

es: Esquema de conductor gig fraudulento

The candidate is offered work as a ride-hailing or delivery driver for a platform (Uber, DiDi, Cabify, Beat, Rappi, DiDi Food). The recruiter provides a 'rented' vehicle on terms that include daily rent, insurance fees and maintenance charges. The arithmetic guarantees the driver ends each week in deficit, binding them through accumulating debt. The vehicle is often stolen or fraudulently registered and is used to extract labour under the threat of criminal complaint.

:IdentityTheftScheme — Identity theft scheme

es: Esquema de robo de identidad

The offer's purpose is to harvest the candidate's personal data (CURP, INE, bank details) to commit downstream fraud against the candidate or third parties. Hiring is never actually intended.

:InPersonLureScheme — In-person lure scheme

es: Esquema de citación presencial

The offer exists solely to bring the candidate to a physical location under the appearance of a hiring event or interview. The real purpose is kidnapping, assault, sexual violence or femicide. Characterised by plausible role, nameless company, multi-use building address, request to come alone and without identification. Documented archetypal case: Edith Guadalupe Valdez Saldívar, Benito Juárez CDMX, April 2026.

Subclass of:FraudScheme

:MLMHealthBeautyScheme — Health / beauty MLM scheme

es: Esquema multinivel de salud y belleza

A multi-level marketing operation centred on nutritional supplements, cosmetics, slimming products or wellness programmes. Presented as an employment or business opportunity, in practice the participant earns only by recruiting further 'distributors' and by purchasing inventory that cannot be sold on the open market. Canonical examples in Latin America include product lines marketed through Facebook groups and WhatsApp chains.

:ModelingAgencyScheme — Modelling agency scheme

es: Esquema de agencia de modelaje

A supposed modelling, acting, influencer or image agency recruits young women, occasionally men, with the promise of bookings abroad. The first 'casting' is used to produce compromising photographs and to extract personal data. Subsequent 'shoots' lead to sexual exploitation, pornographic production without consent or forced escort work. Documented end-state of recruitment on Facebook Marketplace and Instagram DMs in Mexico and Colombia.

Subclass of:FraudScheme

:OnlineCryptoJobScheme — Crypto / forex job scheme

es: Esquema de empleo cripto / forex

Advertised as 'crypto trader', 'forex analyst', 'Web3 community manager' or 'blockchain assistant' with high salary, full remote and no experience required. Often used as cover for pay-to-work (candidate buys a 'training course' or deposits 'starting capital'), identity theft (KYC data collected for money-laundering accounts), or cross-border trafficking into Southeast Asian crypto-scam compounds.

Subclass of:FraudScheme

:OnlineInfluencerSalesScheme — Influencer sales scheme

es: Esquema de ventas como influencer

The candidate is offered a paid role as 'brand ambassador', 'community manager trainee' or 'social media sales associate'. The role requires the candidate to set up their own social accounts, purchase the product catalogue, and 'earn' from commission and from recruiting further ambassadors. In effect a pyramid or MLM structure presented as a creator-economy job. Commonly used by cosmetic, fashion and digital-education brands.

Subclass of:FraudScheme

:OnlineRecruitmentChannel — Online recruitment channel

A digital communication channel used by recruiters to contact candidates. Controlled vocabulary covers Messenger, WhatsApp, Telegram, Marketplace, TikTok, SMS, classifieds and other.

:OrganRemovalRecruitmentScheme — Organ removal recruitment scheme

es: Esquema de reclutamiento para extracción de órganos

The offer promises medical treatment, paid egg donation, or a health procedure. The real purpose is extraction of organs (most commonly kidneys) or tissue for sale on the black market. Traffickers promise US$500 to US$10,000 that the victim often never receives. Documented in detail by the UNODC Assessment Toolkit on Trafficking in Persons for the Purpose of Organ Removal (2015, 60+ pages). Explicitly named as a purpose of trafficking by Article 3(a) of the Palermo Protocol (2000). Recruitment uses social media, word of mouth in vulnerable communities, and classifieds.

Subclass of:FraudScheme

:PalermoAction — Palermo Action

es: Acción (Palermo)

The ACT element of the Palermo Protocol Article 3(a) definition of trafficking in persons: 'the recruitment, transportation, transfer, harbouring or receipt of persons'.

:PalermoMeans — Palermo Means

es: Medio (Palermo)

The MEANS element of the Palermo Protocol Article 3(a) definition: 'the threat or use of force or other forms of coercion, of abduction, of fraud, of deception, of the abuse of power or of a position of vulnerability or of the giving or receiving of payments or benefits to achieve the consent of a person having control over another person'.

Superclass of:MeansAbduction, :MeansAbuseOfPower, :MeansAbuseOfVulnerability, :MeansCoercion, :MeansDeception, :MeansFraud, :MeansPaymentOrBenefit, :MeansThreatOfForce

:PalermoPurpose — Palermo Purpose

es: Finalidad (Palermo)

The PURPOSE element of the Palermo Protocol Article 3(a): 'for the purpose of exploitation', which the Protocol exemplifies as 'the exploitation of the prostitution of others or other forms of sexual exploitation, forced labour or services, slavery or practices similar to slavery, servitude or the removal of organs'.

Superclass of:PurposeForcedLabour, :PurposeOrganRemoval, :PurposeServitude, :PurposeSexualExploitation, :PurposeSlavery

Alignment with HUTRO

Eight classes in RATR-O declare a skos:closeMatch to the corresponding concept in the Human Trafficking Ontology (HUTRO, Tesfahans, BioPortal, 2025). These are: :RecruitmentMethod, :TypeOfExploitation, :VulnerabilityFactor, :TransportationMeans, :CommunicationTool, :DocumentationType, :FalseDocumentation, and :TraffickingRoute. Classes specific to recruitment fraud, online channels, survivor-testimony patterns and Ramona's operational pipeline (for example :RedFlag, :SurvivorTestimonyPattern, :OnlineRecruitmentChannel, :JobOffer) are introduced by RATR-O and have no direct counterpart in HUTRO.
