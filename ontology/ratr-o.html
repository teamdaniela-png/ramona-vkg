Abstract

A domain ontology in OWL 2 QL (DL-Lite) for the detection of fraudulent job offers, labour-recruitment fraud, and human-trafficking recruitment patterns in Latin America. RATR-O models the full continuum of recruitment-based harm: from offers that defraud candidates (identity theft, financial scams, unpaid work) through offers that lead to labour exploitation, through to offers whose purpose is trafficking into forced labour, sexual exploitation, or forced criminality. A single JobOffer can exhibit indicators of any combination of these three outcomes, and RATR-O expresses them as distinct but compatible risk axes. The ontology is designed to operate as the conceptual layer of a Virtual Knowledge Graph over Ramona AI operational data and over open anti-trafficking datasets (CTDC, UNODC), following Xiao, Ding, Cogrel and Calvanese (2019). v1.0 aligns the generalisable fragment of HUTRO (Human Trafficking Ontology, BioPortal, 2025) with Ramona's domain-specific taxonomy of online recruitment fraud in Mexico and Latin America.

v1.9 module: social-media criminal recruitment

Derived from El Colegio de México (2025), Del scroll al “jale”. The four QCA conditions of the report (NR, ECC, AVC, A) are classes of posts; the three recruitment stories are subclasses with the necessary direction axiomatised and the sufficient direction (a conjunction, outside OWL 2 QL) computed in the mapping. Entry points: :SocialMediaItem, :DirectRecruitmentPost, :SocialMediaRecruitmentSignal, :LexiconTerm. Design note: MODULE_SOCIAL_RECRUITMENT.md.

:AgriculturalBondageScheme — Agricultural labour bondage scheme

es: Esquema de servidumbre en trabajo agrícola

Offer of seasonal agricultural work (jornalero, harvest, produce packing) that leads to wage theft, deductions for housing and food that exceed the wage, confiscation of identification documents, and restriction of movement through geographic isolation. Documented by ILO, UNODC and the Polaris Project in the US-Mexico corridor (fresh produce, coffee, tomato) and in internal Mexican migration from southern states to Sinaloa, Sonora and Baja California. The CTDC dataset registers agricultural labour as a distinct exploitation subtype.

Subclass of:FraudScheme
See alsohttps://www.unodc.org/documents/human-trafficking/2015/Recruitment_Fees_Report-Final-22_June_2015_AG_Final.pdf, https://polarisproject.org/

:BeggingRingScheme — Forced begging ring scheme

es: Esquema de mendicidad forzada

Offer of 'help' or 'a job in the city' that leads to forced begging under the control of a ring leader. Common victims are minors, persons with disabilities, and migrants with precarious status. The ring leader confiscates earnings and controls movement. Named explicitly in the UNODC Protocol against Trafficking in Persons glossary and discussed by UNICEF in urban contexts in Mexico, Honduras and Guatemala. Maps to :PurposeForcedLabour or :PurposeSlavery depending on the context.

Subclass of:FraudScheme
See alsohttps://www.unodc.org/unodc/en/human-trafficking/faqs.html

Subclass of:CriminalCultureExaltationPost, :AcousticFramingPost, :CriminalLifeAspirationPost

:CallCenterScamScheme — Call-centre scam employment scheme

es: Esquema de empleo en call center estafador

The candidate is hired as a legitimate customer-service, collections or sales agent in a call centre that is in fact a fraud operation (time-share extortion, fake investment, virtual kidnapping, romance scams). The worker realises they are defrauding clients and attempts to leave, at which point threats, wage withholding or confiscated documents prevent them from doing so. Documented in Mexico, Costa Rica and the Philippines.

Subclass of:FraudScheme

:CentralAmericanMigrantLabourScheme — Central-American migrant labour exploitation scheme

es: Esquema de explotación laboral de migrantes centroamericanos

Offer of work in the United States or northern Mexico, targeted at Guatemalan, Honduran or Salvadoran candidates, that routes the victim via Tapachula and the Mexican migration corridor. On arrival, the victim is placed in construction, agriculture or hospitality under conditions of debt bondage: smuggling fees, document retention, threats of denunciation to immigration authorities. CTDC documents the GTM-USA corridor with 842 victims 2009-2023 and the HND and SLV citizenship clusters. Recognised by UNODC GLOTIP as a principal Latin-American corridor.

Subclass of:DebtBondageTraffickingScheme
See alsohttps://www.ctdatacollaborative.org/page/global-synthetic-dataset, https://www.unodc.org/unodc/data-and-analysis/glotip.html

:ChildRecruitmentCriminalScheme — Child recruitment into criminal organisations

es: Esquema de reclutamiento infantil por grupos delictivos

Recruitment of minors by organised-crime organisations as halcones (lookouts), runners, enforcers, or in the production and logistics of drug trafficking. Extensively documented in Mexico by REDIM (Red por los Derechos de la Infancia en México) in their 2010-2024 series. Recognised by the CNDH and ongoing federal legislative debate. Entry points include offers disguised as 'security', 'warehouse work' or 'deliveries', plus in-school contact and social-media grooming. UNICEF treats recruitment of minors by armed groups as a grave violation.

Subclass of:ForcedCriminalityScheme
Superclass of:GangRecruitmentSchoolsScheme

:ConstructionLabourBondageScheme — Construction labour bondage scheme

es: Esquema de servidumbre en construcción

Offer of construction work abroad or in another state with housing and 'flight to site' included. Upon arrival the worker finds pay deductions for housing, transport and tools that exceed the wage; documents are retained; movement off site is restricted. Documented by ILO in the Gulf States labour corridor (most notoriously Qatar World Cup preparation) and by HRW and Polaris in the US-Mexico corridor for Mexican and Central-American workers. Also attested internally in Mexico for workers displaced from southern states to the northern border.

Subclass of:FraudScheme

:CredibleLethalPattern — Credible-lethal pattern

es: Patrón creíble-letal

A survivor-testimony pattern where the offer itself looks plausible and the risk sits in the logistics of the first contact (nameless WhatsApp, multi-use building, 'come alone', 'no ID', Messenger-to-WhatsApp funnel, personal-data filter). Type B in Ramona's dual-track classification.

:CredibleLethalRedFlag — Credible-lethal red flag (Type B)

The value is in :flagType.

:DebtBondageTraffickingScheme — Debt bondage trafficking scheme

:DomesticWorkerLockInScheme — Domestic worker lock-in scheme

es: Esquema de encierro en trabajo doméstico

The candidate is hired as a live-in domestic worker, caregiver or nanny and then isolated inside the employer's household: documents withheld, phone confiscated or rationed, days-off refused, movement outside the home forbidden. :FishingVesselLabourScheme — Fishing vessel labour scheme

es: Esquema de explotación laboral en pesca

Offer of work on deep-sea fishing vessels with promised wages and return dates. Alignment with HUTRO

Eight classes in RATR-O declare a skos:closeMatch to the corresponding concept in the Human Trafficking Ontology (HUTRO, Tesfahans, BioPortal, 2025). These are: :RecruitmentMethod, :TypeOfExploitation, :VulnerabilityFactor, :TransportationMeans, :CommunicationTool, :DocumentationType, :FalseDocumentation, and :TraffickingRoute. Classes specific to recruitment fraud, online channels, survivor-testimony patterns and Ramona's operational pipeline (for example :RedFlag, :SurvivorTestimonyPattern, :OnlineRecruitmentChannel, :JobOffer) are introduced by RATR-O and have no direct counterpart in HUTRO.
